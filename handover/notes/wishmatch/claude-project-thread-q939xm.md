## Line
The new recommender Labiba chose on 2026-10-21 after the legacy engine's picks disappointed. Plan: https://claude.ai/artifact/NppvUxq4ADjSns3kthJrPi ("Wishmatch: the plan"); the build prompt is `/mnt/project-files/wishmatch/new-thread-prompt.md`. Read both before building.
- What it does: reads the typed request (the "wish"), turns it into the mood words of `services/vibe_vocab.py`, takes about 2,000 well-known candidates (separate film and show pools), asks at most two questions where those candidates really differ, scores them against the 2026-10-08 Haiku mood tags (§10.1) and both tastes, and shows each pick with its reason words.
- Rules: runs beside legacy and TIGER, picked by `recommender.engine: wishmatch | legacy | tiger`; old engines untouched. Reusable core in `packages/wishmatch/` with no app imports (Labiba will lift it out for other projects); movie and TV parts in `services/wishmatch_movies/`. Every weight in a `wishmatch:` section of `recommender_rules.yaml`. Solo = own taste plus the partner's at 0.2; together = both equally, pushing down what either would dislike.
- Models and cost: mood tags for new titles from an open-weight Ollama model on the Mac (free); the wish reader on `mars`'s Ollama container (free); Haiku only for the written taste profiles at Train time (under 1 cent per Train).
- Step 1 done (2026-10-21): `mars` already has all 48,260 mood tags (§10.1). Tag `known` split: films 5,802 well / 20,451 somewhat / 9,601 not; shows 1,292 / 7,868 / 3,246. Ollama is **not running on `mars`** (2026-10-21: no container, the `ollama` service is inactive, nothing on port 11434), so step 4 must first set up an Ollama service, probably as a Homelab service next to Jellyfin, never touching Home Assistant or Matter. Step 2 (PR #99): `packages/wishmatch/` (no app imports; tests in its `tests/`, run by `tests/test_wishmatch_core.py`), `services/wishmatch_movies/` (adapter, `WishmatchMovies` with the legacy `recommend()` signature, `EngineSwitch` used by `/api/recommend`, NL picks and Choose for us), `recommender.engine` in both config files (default `legacy`), `wishmatch:` in `recommender_rules.yaml`, new `/api/rec/movies/recommend`, `/api/rec/movies/wish`, `/api/rec/status`. Adapter contract has one method more than the plan: `items(request)`. Catalogue cache ~150 MB, 2.3 s first load, ~0.1 s per request. The Discover questions pop-up still uses legacy until step 5.
- Step 3 (MovieLens crowd): `training/build_wishmatch_crowd.py` turns `training/data/ml-latest-small` (already on the Mac, linked by TMDB id) into `wishmatch/crowd.json`: for each film, the 30 films its fans also rated highly (3,171 films, 1.2 MB, under a second). The Train button builds it (`ops/mac/nightly.sh`, a failure only warns) and ships it in the model bundle (`cc.py build-bundle --wishmatch`); the server reads `<MODELS_DIR>/wishmatch/crowd.json` and works without it. The package's `crowd.py` scores each candidate by your reactions to the films it neighbours (`weights.crowd: 0.4`), adds up to 300 strong crowd suggestions past the popularity cut, and the card says "fans of X loved it". Films only: shows get no crowd score, so the film/show mix setting keeps their share. ml-latest-small is small (610 people); a bigger MovieLens set is a later option (a download, ask first).
- Step 4 (wish reader): Ollama runs as a Homelab service (`homelab.yaml`, `ollama/ollama:0.40.1`, no ports, 3 GB cap, `OLLAMA_URL=http://ollama:11434` for the app). Idle it sleeps; the model loads on a search and unloads after 5 minutes. Dev gets its own empty copy (Homelab services can't be prod-only), but only prod downloads the model at start (`wish_reader.pull_in: [prod]`, Qwen 2.5 3B, about 1.9 GB), so dev uses the word lookup. The package's `llm.py` asks the model to pick vocabulary words only (no dials: every word it writes costs time on `mars`'s processor); its words are laid over the word lookup's reading as one phrase, its "ruled out" words count only for content warnings and only when the wish says no/not/without. A search waits `wait_seconds: 8`; a slower answer is kept for Show more and repeats. `word_question()` words questions for step 5 (template fallback). On the Mac (gemma3:4b) a reading took 4-8 s; **not yet measured on `mars`**: check `GET /api/rec/status` (`wish_reader.last_seconds`) after deploy and tune `wait_seconds` / the model.
- Step 5 (questions): with `engine: wishmatch`, `/api/discover/start` makes a `WishmatchSession` (`services/wishmatch_movies/session.py`, same methods as `MoodSession`; the mood-map pop-up stays for legacy and as the fallback). The package's `questions.py` looks at the top 40 picks and asks about the mood dial they spread on most (low / high end) or the 2-3 feelings or occasions that split them (silly / witty), never about what the wish already said, at most 2, none when the picks agree. Options carry two example titles each; the model words the question (6 s limit, else "Which feels right: A or B?"). An answer counts half as much as a typed word (`questions.answer_weight`), via `Wish.weights`. The fantasy dial ("grounded realism or another world") wins often; watch it in step 7.
- Step 6 (tagging model and taste profiles):
  - Bake-off (`utils/mood_tag_ollama.py bakeoff`; report `reports/mood_tag_bakeoff.json` on the Mac, git-ignored): 100 Haiku-tagged titles re-tagged on the Mac. Gemma 3 4B won narrowly (closest on feelings; dials off by 0.12 on 0-1, about 1.2 points on Haiku's 0-10; feeling words share about 2 of 5; occasion words rarely match) over Qwen 3 8B; Llama 3.1 8B was behind and slow; Gemma 3 12B was stopped (about 12 min a title, too big for 16 GB). Only Gemma 3 4B is kept in the Mac's Ollama. It takes 40-60 s a title, so the Train button tags new titles for at most `nightly.mood_tag_minutes` (default 60) a run, best-known first, into the snapshot so the catalogue patch carries them to `mars`; the tag records `model`. Haiku is the better-quality option for new titles (about 1 cent per 100).
  - Taste profiles (`utils/wishmatch_profiles.py`, table `wishmatch_profiles`, schema **v22**): by default the Mac's Ollama model (`wishmatch.tagging_model`, Gemma 3 4B, free, about a minute a person) writes a short summary; the liked / avoided feeling and occasion words are worked out from the ratings themselves (more common among loved, or disliked, titles than among all rated). `--haiku` uses Claude Haiku instead (costs money: estimate only without `--yes`). Train makes them every run unless `nightly.wishmatch_profiles: false`, and pushes them to the server (`--push`). `GET` / `PUT /api/rec/movies/profile/{person}` read and edit them; a hand edit is never overwritten. Declared likes and dislikes count at `taste.declared_weight: 0.3`. Gabriele had rated no dislikes yet (2026-10-24), so his profile has no avoided words.
  - Seen titles: alone, anything the viewer has seen is left out; together, what both have seen is left out and what one has seen is pushed below every unseen title (`people.seen_penalty: 1.5` x share who saw it).
- Step 7 (side-by-side test): `utils/compare_engines.py` runs 30 requests (laugh, rough day, korean thriller, 80s, hidden gem sci-fi, mood cards, films-and-shows mixes, alone and together, ...) through legacy and Wishmatch on a copy of the catalogue and checks each top 10 (right genre / country / years, well known, foreign only if top tier, nothing already seen, films and shows both shown); tests in `tests/test_compare_engines.py`. Result 2026-10-24: legacy 100/104 checks, Wishmatch 110/111 (word lookup only, no Ollama). Fixes from it: decades typed in the wish filter the years ("80s"); "sci-fi" reads as Science Fiction; genre hints pull softly (`weights.genre_hint: 0.4`, legacy `genre_hints` plus Wishmatch-only `wishmatch.genre_hints`, e.g. tearjerker -> Drama); a rating from few votes counts less (`quality_votes: {movie: 1000, tv: 300}`). Both engines still fail "bollywood" on how well known the picks are (few high-vote Hindi films in the catalogue). Page: https://claude.ai/artifact/CVjgz3bngxDR7MdEf3fxEa
